About peptide.diy
What does peptide.diy provide?
We publish dosage protocols, mixing guides, and reference material for peptide research. Everything is written for people who want numbers they can verify — drawn from clinical literature, FDA labels, and primary research, not repackaged forum posts or vendor sales pages.
Can I treat this site as medical advice?
No. peptide.diy is educational only. Nothing here diagnoses, treats, or replaces advice from a licensed clinician. If you are making health decisions, speak with a qualified professional first.
Does peptide.diy sell peptides or supplies?
No — we do not sell, ship, or supply any compounds or equipment. We are an information site only. When we mention vendors, it is to point readers toward sources we consider trustworthy and safe, not because we have a commercial relationship with them.
What's the difference between singles, blends, and stacks?
Single peptides are one compound per vial — for example, a 5 mg BPC-157 vial. Blends arrive pre-combined: two or more peptides already mixed at a fixed ratio in one vial, such as a CJC-1295/Ipamorelin blend. Stacks are coordinated protocols that use separate vials on a shared schedule — you mix and dose each compound independently, but follow a combined plan.
How can I request a protocol that isn't listed yet?
Send a message through our Contact page. Include the peptide name, vial size, and anything else that matters for dosing. We add new pages based on demand and available research.
Mixing & Measuring Doses
What does reconstitution mean?
It is the step where you add liquid — usually bacteriostatic water — to freeze-dried peptide powder so you can measure doses. How much water you add sets the concentration, which determines how many millilitres (or syringe units) you need per dose. Our Reconstitution Guide walks through the full process.
Why is bacteriostatic water used?
BAC water is sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol added as a preservative. That preservative slows bacterial growth after the vial is punctured, which is why a properly stored solution can stay usable for several weeks instead of hours.
How do I choose how much BAC water to add?
It depends on vial size and the dose you want to draw comfortably. Our protocol pages recommend a BAC volume for each compound — often 1, 2, or 3 mL — chosen so the maths stays straightforward. For a custom amount, use the Dosage Calculator for injections, moisturiser creams, or nasal sprays.
When should I use IU instead of mg?
Milligrams (mg) measure mass — how much powder is in the vial. International Units (IU) measure biological activity and appear on a handful of compounds, notably HGH and HCG. For growth hormone, a common reference is roughly 3 IU per 1 mg of somatropin, though the exact conversion can vary by product. Most peptides on this site are dosed in mg or mcg, not IU.
How do mcg and mg relate to each other?
There are 1,000 micrograms (mcg) in one milligram (mg). A 250 mcg dose equals 0.25 mg. Because many peptides are active at microgram levels, always double-check the unit on the label and in the protocol — mixing up mg and mcg can mean a thousand-fold error.
Which syringes work for peptide injections?
U-100 insulin syringes are the standard for subcutaneous peptide injections. On U-100 markings, 100 units equal 1 mL — so each unit is 0.01 mL. Half-millilitre and one-millilitre barrels are both common. For injection, topical, and nasal deployment, see our Deployment Methods guide.
Storage & Shelf Life
How should lyophilised powder be stored?
Keep dry powder cool, dark, and away from moisture. Refrigeration at 2–8°C (36–46°F) is best for long-term stability; some compounds tolerate freezing while still lyophilised. Peptide-specific guidance is in our Storage Guide.
Where does a reconstituted vial go?
Refrigerate immediately at 2–8°C (36–46°F). Store upright, handle gently, and avoid shaking the solution. Do not freeze liquid peptide — ice formation can damage the peptide structure.
How long is a mixed vial good for?
With bacteriostatic water and proper refrigeration, most solutions remain usable for about 25–30 days. A few peptides stay stable longer. If you reconstituted with plain sterile water instead, treat the vial as single-use and discard within 24–48 hours — there is no preservative to stop contamination.

Safety & Legal
Are research peptides legal where I live?
Rules differ by country, state, and compound. In the US, many peptides are marketed for laboratory research only. Several — including semaglutide, HGH, and HCG — are prescription drugs when used clinically. Federal law also restricts non-medical HGH distribution (21 U.S.C. § 333(e)). Verify your local regulations before acquiring or using any compound.
Will peptides show up on a sports drug test?
Many peptides are banned in tested sport under WADA rules — including growth hormone, GH secretagogues (CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, MK-677, and others), and GH fragments. Athletes in governed competitions should review the current WADA Prohibited List before using any peptide.
What side effects should I know about?
Effects depend entirely on the compound. Injection-site irritation, headache, nausea, and fatigue appear across several peptides. GH-related agents may cause fluid retention, joint discomfort, or shifts in blood sugar. Each protocol page lists compound-specific risks drawn from clinical sources — there is no single answer that covers every peptide.
Is it safe to run more than one peptide at a time?
Combination use is common in research, and our Stacks and Blends sections cover supported pairings — but every added compound increases interaction and monitoring complexity. Stack pages note where evidence is stronger versus more speculative. Discuss combinations with a healthcare professional before starting.
