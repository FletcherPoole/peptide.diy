Insulin Syringes: The Standard Tool

Nearly all peptide protocols use insulin syringes. They are designed for small, precise volumes and have thin-gauge needles (typically 29G, 30G, or 31G) that are appropriate for subcutaneous injection. Insulin syringes are widely available, inexpensive, and come pre-attached with a fixed needle, which means less dead space compared to syringes with detachable needles.

Insulin syringes are labeled by two characteristics: their total capacity (how much liquid they hold) and their unit scale (what the tick marks represent). Understanding the scale is where most confusion starts.

Syringe Types and Scales

U-100 Syringes (1 mL)

The most common syringe used in peptide research. A U-100 1 mL syringe has 100 unit markings, where 100 units = 1 mL. Each single unit mark = 0.01 mL.

  • Total capacity: 1 mL (100 units)
  • Smallest graduation: typically 2 units
  • Best for: doses requiring 10 to 100 units (0.10 to 1.00 mL)

U-100 Syringes (0.5 mL)

A half-milliliter version. Still uses the same scale (100 units per mL), but only holds 50 units. The markings are physically more spread out, making small volumes easier to read accurately.

  • Total capacity: 0.5 mL (50 units)
  • Smallest graduation: typically 1 unit
  • Best for: doses requiring 5 to 50 units

U-100 Syringes (0.3 mL)

The smallest common insulin syringe. Holds 30 units on the U-100 scale. Gives you the most precise visual reading for very small doses.

  • Total capacity: 0.3 mL (30 units)
  • Smallest graduation: 0.5 units (half-unit markings)
  • Best for: doses requiring 1 to 30 units

U-50 Syringes

Less common in the peptide space. A U-50 syringe has 50 units = 0.5 mL. Each unit mark = 0.01 mL — the same volume per unit as U-100. Because U-50 and U-100 (0.5 mL) syringes both hold 0.5 mL with marks representing 0.01 mL each, the volume drawn at any given unit marking is the same.

U-20 Syringes

Used primarily in veterinary contexts. A U-20 syringe has 20 units = 0.2 mL, with each unit = 0.01 mL. Widest spacing between marks — easiest to read for tiny volumes.

How to Read a Syringe

  1. Identify the syringe type. Check the packaging or barrel print. It will say "U-100" and indicate total capacity.
  2. Understand what the lines mean. Numbered markings typically appear at every 10 units, with shorter lines between. On 0.5 mL syringes, lines are typically single-unit increments.
  3. Read at the plunger tip. Read the measurement at the top edge of the plunger's rubber stopper. Hold the syringe at eye level for an accurate reading.
  4. Account for air bubbles. After drawing, hold the syringe with needle pointing up, flick the barrel, expel air, and re-draw if needed.

The Three Unit Systems

Weight: mg and mcg

Milligrams (mg) and micrograms (mcg) measure how much peptide by mass. This is what the vial label shows and what your target dose is expressed in.

Critical Rule 1 mg = 1,000 mcg. A protocol calling for 250 mcg is NOT the same as 250 mg. Confusing the two creates a 1,000-fold dosing error.

Volume: mL and syringe units

Milliliters (mL) and syringe units measure how much liquid you draw. These tell you nothing about the peptide content until you know the concentration. Syringe "units" are purely volume marks — they are not International Units.

Potency: International Units (IU)

International Units measure biological activity for specific peptides where potency has been standardized. The two peptides where IU is commonly used are:

  • HGH (somatropin): approximately 3 IU per 1 mg
  • HCG: IU defined by bioassay, not a simple weight conversion. Vials labeled by total IU (e.g., 5,000 IU)

Most peptides do not use IU. If your protocol is for BPC-157, semaglutide, Ipamorelin, or most other peptides, you are working with mg/mcg only and IU does not apply.

Conversion Math

Every dosing calculation follows the same three-step process:

Step 1: Calculate Concentration Concentration = Total peptide in vial ÷ BAC water added. Example: 5 mg + 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL
Step 2: Calculate Dose Volume Dose volume (mL) = Target dose ÷ Concentration. Example: 0.25 mg ÷ 2.5 mg/mL = 0.10 mL
Step 3: Convert to Syringe Units Syringe units = Dose volume (mL) × 100 (for U-100). Example: 0.10 mL × 100 = 10 units

Common Mistakes

Confusing syringe "units" with IU"Inject 2 IU of HGH" does NOT mean draw to the "2" mark. You must calculate volume based on concentration.
Confusing mg and mcg250 mcg ≠ 250 mg. Always double-check the unit abbreviation.
Reading the wrong part of the plungerRead at the top flat edge of the rubber, not the bottom curve. Reading at the wrong point introduces a 1–2 unit error per draw.
Not accounting for air bubblesA 5-unit air bubble in a 15-unit draw means you have only 10 units of actual liquid.
Using the wrong syringe for the dose volumeIf your dose is 5 units, trying to read that on a 1 mL syringe is inaccurate. Switch to a 0.5 mL or 0.3 mL syringe.

Needle Gauge and Length

Gauge (thickness)

  • 29G: Standard. Slightly thicker, easier to draw viscous solutions. Good all-around choice.
  • 30G: Thinner than 29G. Slightly less tissue disruption. Most popular for peptide use.
  • 31G: Thinnest common option. Least discomfort but can be slow to draw thicker solutions.

Length

  • 1/2 inch (12.7 mm): The most common length for subcutaneous injection. Appropriate for most body types.
  • 5/16 inch (8 mm): Shorter option. Preferred by some for leaner body types or shallow SubQ injection.

The standard recommendation in most protocols is 30G × 1/2 inch.

Measurement Checklist

  1. Confirm the protocol dose and verify whether it is in mg, mcg, or IU.
  2. Check your reconstitution concentration (total peptide ÷ BAC water volume).
  3. Calculate the dose volume in mL (target dose ÷ concentration).
  4. Convert mL to syringe units (mL × 100 for U-100).
  5. Select the appropriate syringe size for that dose volume.
  6. Verify the syringe is U-100 by checking the barrel or packaging.
  7. Draw the liquid and check for air bubbles.
  8. Read at the top flat edge of the plunger rubber, at eye level.
  9. Confirm the reading matches your calculated syringe units before proceeding.
