Choosing a Method

Most peptide protocols assume subcutaneous injection — reconstitute with BAC water, draw with an insulin syringe, inject into fatty tissue. That is the default for compounds where injectable bioavailability is well understood.

Two other routes appear frequently in research and community practice:

  • Topical moisturiser — reconstituted peptide blended into a plain cream base (common with GHK-Cu). Not injected.
  • Nasal spray — peptide reconstituted in BAC water, diluted with sterile saline, loaded into a nasal sprayer.

Subcutaneous Injection

Nearly all injectable peptide protocols use U-100 insulin syringes — thin needles (29G–31G), pre-attached, designed for small precise volumes. Common sites: lower abdomen, outer thigh, upper arm.

Reading a U-100 syringe

On a U-100 scale, 100 units = 1 mL. Each unit mark = 0.01 mL. Syringe "units" measure liquid volume only — they are not International Units (IU).

  • 1 mL barrel — holds up to 100 units. Standard for most doses.
  • 0.5 mL barrel — holds 50 units. Markings are more spread out; easier for small draws.
  • 0.3 mL barrel — holds 30 units. Best precision for very small volumes.

How to read the plunger

  1. Confirm the barrel says U-100.
  2. Read at the top flat edge of the plunger rubber, at eye level.
  3. Flick out air bubbles before confirming your final reading.

Needle size

Most protocols recommend 30G × ½ inch for subcutaneous injection. 29G draws viscous solutions more easily; 31G is thinnest and tends to feel gentler.

Injection math (three steps) Concentration = peptide in vial ÷ BAC water added · Dose volume (mL) = target dose ÷ concentration · Syringe units = dose volume × 100

Topical Cream (Moisturiser)

Some peptides — especially GHK-Cu — are mixed into a plain, fragrance-free moisturiser after reconstitution in BAC water. The cream is applied to skin; it is not injected.

Basic process

  1. Reconstitute the peptide vial with BAC water as usual.
  2. Measure a set weight of plain moisturiser base (grams).
  3. Blend the reconstituted liquid into the moisturiser using clean technique.
  4. Store the mixed cream refrigerated. Label with date.

How much cream per application?

Most people use a pea-sized dab — roughly 0.25–0.5 g of cream per application. A fingertip unit (FTU) is often cited as ~0.5 g. Use that as a visual guide, not a syringe measurement.

Peptide content per dab depends on your mix: concentration in the final cream (mcg per gram) × grams applied. The calculator's topical mode handles this once you enter vial amount, BAC water, moisturiser weight, and target dose.

Topical ≠ injectable Never inject a peptide prepared for topical use. Compounds formulated or mixed for skin application are not sterile for injection even if reconstituted with BAC water.

Nasal Spray

Nasal delivery is used for certain peptides where mucosal absorption is the goal. The typical workflow: reconstitute in BAC water, dilute with sterile saline, load into a clean nasal spray bottle.

Basic process

  1. Reconstitute peptide in BAC water.
  2. Add sterile saline to reach your target total volume and concentration.
  3. Transfer to a nasal spray bottle (or use a metered sprayer you have calibrated).
  4. Refrigerate after mixing. Label with date.

Liquid per spray

Every sprayer delivers a different volume per pump — commonly 0.05–0.15 mL. Peptide delivered per spray = final concentration (mcg/mL) × mL per spray. Know your sprayer's output before trusting a dose schedule.

Total liquid volume = BAC water + saline added. The calculator's nasal mode lets you enter saline volume and liquid per spray alongside your target dose.

Why dilute with saline? BAC water preserves the vial for repeated punctures, but many nasal protocols dilute further with saline to lower concentration and match the small volume a sprayer delivers per pump.

Units & Conversion Math

Weight: mg and mcg

Milligrams and micrograms measure peptide mass — what the vial label shows and what most doses specify.

Critical rule 1 mg = 1,000 mcg. Confusing mg and mcg creates a 1,000-fold dosing error.

Volume: mL and syringe units

Millilitres and U-100 syringe units measure liquid volume. They tell you nothing about peptide content until you know the concentration.

Potency: International Units (IU)

IU measures biological activity for specific compounds — primarily HGH (~3 IU per 1 mg somatropin) and HCG (labelled by total IU). Most peptides use mg or mcg, not IU.

Common Mistakes

Confusing syringe units with IU"Inject 2 IU of HGH" does not mean draw to the 2 mark. Calculate volume from concentration.
Confusing mg and mcgAlways verify the unit on the protocol and vial label.
Injecting topical mixPeptide blended into moisturiser is for skin application only.
Guessing nasal spray outputWithout knowing mL per pump, you cannot know mcg per spray.
Air bubbles in syringe drawsA bubble displaces liquid — your actual dose is lower than the reading shows.

Checklists by Method

Injection

  1. Confirm dose unit (mg, mcg, or IU).
  2. Calculate concentration and dose volume.
  3. Convert mL to U-100 units (× 100).
  4. Use a fresh syringe. Draw, debubble, verify reading at the plunger top edge.

Topical moisturiser

  1. Reconstitute with BAC water. Note concentration (mg/mL).
  2. Weigh moisturiser base (grams). Blend with clean technique.
  3. Calculate mcg per gram in the final mix.
  4. Apply a pea-sized amount (~0.25–0.5 g). Store cream refrigerated.

Nasal spray

  1. Reconstitute in BAC water, then add sterile saline to target volume.
  2. Confirm your sprayer's mL per pump.
  3. Calculate mcg delivered per spray at your concentration.
  4. Compare to target dose. Adjust dilution if needed. Refrigerate after mixing.
