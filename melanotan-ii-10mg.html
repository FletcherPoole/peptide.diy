Melanotan II 10mg Dosage Protocol

Synthetic melanocortin agonist studied for skin pigmentation, with noted MC4R-mediated side effects in early human trials. Subcutaneous and nasal routes.

At a Glance
  • Vial: 10 mg Melanotan II lyophilised powder
  • Mix: 2 mL BAC water → 5 mg/mL (5,000 mcg/mL)
  • Unit maths: 1 U-100 unit = 0.01 mL = 50 mcg
  • Recommended approach: dose only on tanning days — no daily loading phase
  • Start: 50–75 mcg subQ, assess response, then +25 mcg every 3 tanning sessions
  • UV timing: sensible sun or UV within 1 hour of injection (pre-tan, not night-only)
  • Working dose: many users reach 250–500 mcg per tanning session over weeks (not days)
  • Maintenance: ~250 mcg once weekly plus minimal UV
  • Frequency: every 2nd or 3rd day on tanning days only; skip MT-II on rest days

Cycle

Recommended: tanning-day protocol. This is the approach widely regarded as the best balance of colour, side effects, and mole/freckle risk in long-running community write-ups (including More Plates More Dates). It replaces the old “daily loading phase” still copied across many sites.

Core rules
  • No loading period. Do not inject every day for weeks “to build up” melanin while skipping UV.
  • Tanning days only. Inject subQ only on days you will get UV exposure (sun or tanning bed).
  • UV within 1 hour. After the shot, get UV while melanin production is active. Night-only dosing without UV tends to darken freckles, moles, and genital skin unevenly.
  • Start tiny. First dose 50–75 mcg. Jumping straight to 500 mcg is a common reason for nausea and new freckles.

Why pre-tan dosing beats night-only

MT-II mainly drives melanogenesis (new melanin). Visible “beach bronze” also needs UV oxidation of that melanin (especially UVA). Inject → UV within an hour lets new pigment form where skin needs protection, then oxidise for an even tan. A big daily load with UV only on weekends often tans exposed areas hard and leaves patchy colour elsewhere.

UV progression: start with very short exposure (1–2 minutes in a bed, or brief sensible sun). Add ~1 minute per tanning session as dose and melanin density rise. Fair skin or freckle-prone users should stay conservative.

Schedule: inject and tan every 2nd or 3rd day (or less often if colour builds quickly). Rest days = no MT-II, no forced tanning.

Use 30- or 50-unit syringes for micro-doses under 5 units; they are easier to read than a full 1 mL syringe.

Session checklist

  1. Optional: antihistamine 15–45 minutes before (flushing/nausea).
  2. Draw dose, inject subQ (abdomen, thigh, or upper arm). Rotate sites.
  3. Within 1 hour: controlled UV exposure. Use appropriate protection; do not burn.
  4. Log dose, UV duration, and any side effects before increasing.

Alternative: daily loading protocol

Older protocols use daily subQ injections for 6–8 weeks whether or not you tan that day. Phase I trials used every-other-day mg/kg dosing (Dorr et al., 1996). Community experience suggests daily loading without matched UV increases nausea, uneven pigmentation, and freckling. If you use it anyway, pair each injection with UV and still titrate slowly.

Dosing & Reconstitution

Reconstitution (2 mL standard)

Recommended BAC water: 2.0 mL → 10 mg ÷ 2 mL = 5 mg/mL (5,000 mcg/mL)

  1. Swab rubber stoppers on peptide and BAC vials. Let dry.
  2. Draw 2.0 mL bacteriostatic water (1 mL at a time if using a 1 mL syringe).
  3. Inject slowly down the inside wall of the peptide vial. Do not squirt directly onto the powder.
  4. Swirl or roll gently until clear. Do not shake hard.
  5. Label with date and BAC volume. Refrigerate immediately at 2–8 °C.

Formula: dose volume (mL) = desired mcg ÷ 5,000. Syringe units = dose volume × 100.

Examples at 5 mg/mL: 50 mcg = 1 unit · 250 mcg = 5 units · 500 mcg = 10 units.

With BAC, use reconstituted solution within about 1–2 weeks refrigerated. See the Storage Guide.

Injectable dosing

Subcutaneous injection on tanning days only. Get UV within an hour of the shot for even colour. Injecting at night without same-day UV is a common cause of patchy freckling and mole darkening.

Inject slowly. Fresh needle each time; do not reuse. See Deployment Methods · Injection.

Dose ceiling Phase I work explored ~1–2 mg/day in weight-based trials. Case reports document systemic toxicity after far higher single doses. Even on tanning days, do not chase high daily totals.

Alternative Protocols

Nasal spray (DIY)

Community protocols reconstitute with 2 mL BAC (5 mg/mL), then dilute into a nasal spray bottle for non-injectable delivery. Absorption and dose per spray vary by device and technique.

  1. Reconstitute 10 mg with 2 mL BAC → 5 mg/mL in the peptide vial.
  2. Add 6 mL BAC to an empty nasal spray bottle (8 mL total volume).
  3. Transfer the 2 mL peptide solution into the spray bottle → 10 mg in 8 mL ≈ 1.25 mg/mL.
  4. If each spray delivers ~0.1 mL, one spray ≈ 125 mcg.

Premade nasal spray kits

Some suppliers ship peptide powder with a pre-filled spray bottle. Follow kit instructions: add powder to the bottle, roll gently to mix, then dose by spray count. Typical community bands: low 2 sprays (~200 mcg), medium 3–5 sprays (~300–500 mcg), high 6–10 sprays (up to ~1,000 mcg), depending on kit concentration.

Melanotan I (MT-I) vs Melanotan II

MT-I (afamelanotide) is a linear α-MSH analogue with a longer clinical history in photoprotection trials and an approved implant form in some countries. MT-II is a shorter cyclic analogue with stronger melanotropic potency in vitro and more reported MC4R side effects (nausea, erections) in human Phase I work. If MT-II side effects are too harsh, MT-I is sometimes chosen instead.

With UV exposure

The tanning-day protocol depends on UV. Phase I showed pigmentation from MT-II injections alone (Dorr et al., 1996), but a deep, even bronze usually needs controlled UV timed with dosing. Without UV, many users get a yellow-grey cast rather than golden brown.

What Is Melanotan II

Melanotan II (MT-II) is a synthetic cyclic heptapeptide analogue of α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH). It binds melanocortin receptors, especially MC1R on melanocytes (melanin production) and MC4R in the central nervous system (appetite, autonomic effects, sexual function).

Early Phase I studies in healthy volunteers reported increased skin pigmentation after short subcutaneous courses, alongside dose-dependent nausea and, in men, spontaneous erections linked to MC4R activation (Dorr et al., 1996). MT-II is sold as a research chemical; it is not approved as a medicine for tanning or any other indication in most countries.

Supplementary Notes

Maintenance after desired colour

After building colour with tanning-day dosing, many users hold a dark tan on ~250 mcg once weekly plus brief UV. Pigmentation can fade slowly over months even without maintenance; weekly top-ups are optional, not mandatory.

Can MT-II be used alone?

Yes. It does not require a stack partner for melanotropic activity. It is not a gonadal steroid and does not suppress testosterone production in the way AAS do; PCT is not applicable.

Tanning without deliberate UV

MT-II alone can shift skin tone slightly, but this is the weakest approach and the one most linked to uneven mole and freckle darkening. The recommended protocol always pairs injection with same-day UV.

Side effects and mitigation

Common (dose-dependent): facial flushing, nausea, mild fatigue, reduced appetite, increased libido, spontaneous erections in men, darkening or new prominence of moles and freckles, injection-site irritation.

Mitigation (anecdotal / clinical adjuncts):

  • Antihistamine (e.g. diphenhydramine) or ginger root 15–45 minutes before dosing may reduce flushing and nausea.
  • Ondansetron is used by some users for nausea; prescription-only in the UK.
  • Face flushing often fades within an hour as tolerance builds; lower dose beats pushing through.
  • Use the tanning-day protocol: fewer doses, less total peptide, less nausea than daily loading.

Serious: systemic toxicity and rhabdomyolysis reported after very high single doses (Nelson et al., 2012). Regulatory bodies warn against unlicensed melanotan injections (HSE Ireland).

Moles, skin checks, and cancer risk

MT-II stimulates melanocytes. Monitor existing moles and seek dermatology review for new or changing lesions. Melanotan use has appeared in case literature alongside skin cancer diagnoses; UV overexposure (especially tanning beds) is a established carcinogen (PMID 24355990). Increased melanin can be photoprotective in theory (PMID 7983590), but unapproved injectables plus reckless UV is a poor risk trade.

White patches that will not tan (tinea versicolor)

Hypopigmented spots that stay pale during a tan may be tinea versicolor (Malassezia overgrowth), not “missed” melanotan effect. Antifungal washes (ketoconazole) or oral antifungals for severe cases are standard dermatology approaches.

Sport and WADA

Check the current WADA Prohibited List for your sport and federation. Do not assume legality in tested sport without verifying.

Why start low?

Phase I subjects had nausea and somnolence before higher mg/kg levels were tested. Community reports consistently tie freckling, mole darkening, and nausea to starting too high or running a daily load without UV. The tanning-day approach exists to avoid that pattern.

Research

Phase I tanning and safety

Dorr et al. (1996) escalated subQ MT-II in three male volunteers. Pigmentation increased on face, upper body, and buttock after five low every-other-day doses over two weeks. Nausea was common; the authors recommended 0.025 mg/kg/day for subsequent Phase I work (PubMed).

Maintenance dosing

Clinical and dermatology summaries describe transitioning from daily loading to 1–2 weekly injections to sustain pigmentation (DermNet NZ).

MC4R effects

Human work links MT-II to penile erection and sexual motivation via melanocortin pathways (Wessells et al., 2000), consistent with yawning/stretching and erections noted in Phase I.

Toxicity

Case report: subcutaneous MT-II associated with sympathomimetic symptoms and rhabdomyolysis (Nelson et al., 2012).

Research vs anecdote The tanning-day protocol is community-derived (widely summarised by More Plates More Dates and others). Published Phase I data used every-other-day mg/kg dosing, not modern microgram loading charts.

Beyond Tanning

MT-II is discussed online for effects beyond pigmentation. Human evidence is thin outside melanotropic and MC4R endpoints.

Libido and erectile function

Research: MC4R agonism and human MT-II studies report erection and desire effects (Wessells et al., 2000; Dorr et al., 1996).

Anecdotal: increased libido and erection frequency at moderate doses; erectile dysfunction reported after extreme overdoses.

Appetite and body composition

Research: MC4R signalling influences appetite in broader melanocortin literature; MT-II Phase I noted reduced appetite in some subjects.

Anecdotal: mild fat loss or appetite suppression during loading. Not an approved weight-loss drug.

Mood and cognition

Anecdotal: reports of improved mood or calmness. Controlled human data for these claims are lacking.

Neurodevelopment / autism (preclinical only)

Research: Rodent work reports behavioural effects relevant to autism spectrum models (PMID 30629642). This does not translate to human treatment evidence.

Anecdotal: online claims about “reversing autism” are not supported by clinical trials. Treat as speculation.
