Peptide Research Glossary

Definitions for 35+ terms used across our protocols and guides.

A
Amino Acid
The molecular building blocks of peptides and proteins. There are 20 standard amino acids; peptides are chains of 2–50 amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
Analogue
A synthetic compound structurally similar to a naturally occurring molecule, designed to mimic or modify its biological activity. Most research peptides are analogues of endogenous peptides.
B
BAC Water (Bacteriostatic Water)
Sterile water containing 0.9% benzyl alcohol as a preservative. The standard diluent for peptide reconstitution. Inhibits bacterial growth, allowing multi-dose use over 25–30 days.
Bioavailability
The fraction of an administered dose that reaches systemic circulation in active form. Subcutaneous peptide injection typically yields higher bioavailability than oral administration due to first-pass metabolism.
BPC-157
Body Protection Compound-157. A 15-amino acid peptide derived from a protective protein found in gastric juice. Widely researched for tissue repair, wound healing, and anti-inflammatory effects.
C
Certificate of Analysis (COA)
A document provided by a vendor confirming the purity and identity of a compound, typically via HPLC or mass spectrometry. A COA from a third-party lab is important but does not guarantee pharmaceutical-grade quality or sterility.
Concentration
The amount of peptide dissolved per unit volume of solution. Calculated as: Total peptide ÷ BAC water volume (e.g., 5 mg ÷ 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL). Determines the injection volume required for each dose.
CJC-1295
A modified GHRH(1-29) analogue. Available in two forms: With DAC (longer half-life, ~1 week) and No DAC / Modified GRF(1-29) (shorter half-life, ~30 minutes). Often combined with Ipamorelin.
D
DAC (Drug Affinity Complex)
A modification added to CJC-1295 that allows it to bind to albumin in the bloodstream, extending its half-life from ~30 minutes to approximately 1 week.
Diluent
The liquid used to dissolve lyophilized peptide powder. Most commonly bacteriostatic water (BAC water). Other options include sterile water and normal saline, each with different shelf-life implications.
Dose-Response Curve
The relationship between dose and biological effect. Peptides typically show a threshold dose below which there is no effect, an optimal dose range, and a plateau above which additional dosing provides diminishing or adverse effects.
G–H
GH (Growth Hormone)
A protein hormone (191 amino acids) produced by the anterior pituitary gland. Stimulates growth, cell reproduction, and regeneration. Often discussed as somatropin (the pharmaceutical form) or HGH 191AA (research form).
GHRH (Growth Hormone Releasing Hormone)
A hypothalamic peptide that stimulates the pituitary to release GH. GHRH analogues like CJC-1295 and Sermorelin work by mimicking this signal.
GHRP (Growth Hormone Releasing Peptide)
A class of peptides that stimulate GH release through the ghrelin receptor pathway. Examples: GHRP-2, GHRP-6, Ipamorelin. Often combined with GHRH analogues for synergistic GH stimulation.
GLP-1 (Glucagon-Like Peptide-1)
An incretin hormone released by intestinal L-cells in response to food. Stimulates insulin secretion, suppresses glucagon, slows gastric emptying, and reduces appetite. GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide) mimic this action.
Half-Life
The time required for the concentration of a compound in the body to decrease by 50%. Influences dosing frequency — compounds with short half-lives require more frequent injections than those with long half-lives.
I–L
IM (Intramuscular) Injection
Injection directly into muscle tissue. Some peptides (like HCG) specify IM administration. Requires a longer needle than subcutaneous injection and results in faster absorption.
Ipamorelin
A pentapeptide GHRP that stimulates GH release with high selectivity and minimal effect on cortisol or prolactin. Widely researched; commonly combined with CJC-1295.
IU (International Unit)
A unit of biological potency used for specific compounds (like HGH and HCG) where potency is defined by biological effect rather than mass. Not the same as syringe "units." For HGH, approximately 3 IU = 1 mg.
Lyophilization
Freeze-drying. The process of removing water from a substance while frozen under vacuum, creating a stable dry powder (lyophilized or lyophilisate). Research peptides arrive in lyophilized form and must be reconstituted before use.
M–P
mcg (Microgram)
A unit of mass equal to one-thousandth of a milligram (1 mg = 1,000 mcg). Many peptides are dosed in micrograms. Often written as µg.
mg (Milligram)
A unit of mass equal to one-thousandth of a gram. Vial labels typically express total peptide content in milligrams (e.g., "BPC-157 5mg").
mL (Milliliter)
A unit of volume equal to one-thousandth of a liter. The same as 1 cc (cubic centimeter). On a U-100 syringe, 1 mL = 100 syringe units.
Peptide Bond
The covalent bond formed between two amino acids, linking the carboxyl group of one to the amino group of another. This is the connection that forms the backbone of a peptide chain.
Protocol
A standardized plan specifying the dose, frequency, route of administration, reconstitution method, and duration for using a specific peptide or combination of peptides.
R–Z
Receptor
A molecular structure (typically a protein) on or inside a cell that binds to a specific peptide or other ligand, triggering a biological response. Peptide effects are mediated through receptor binding.
Reconstitution
The process of dissolving lyophilized peptide powder into a liquid diluent (typically BAC water) to create an injectable solution. See our full Reconstitution Guide.
SubQ / Subcutaneous
Referring to the tissue layer just beneath the skin. Subcutaneous injection delivers the peptide into this fatty tissue layer, from which it is absorbed into the bloodstream. The most common route for peptide administration.
TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4 Fragment)
A synthetic fragment of the naturally occurring protein Thymosin Beta-4. Researched for its roles in cell migration, wound healing, anti-inflammation, and cardiovascular protection.
U-100 Syringe
The most common insulin syringe. The scale where 100 units = 1 mL, meaning each syringe unit = 0.01 mL. Available in 1 mL (100 unit), 0.5 mL (50 unit), and 0.3 mL (30 unit) capacities.
